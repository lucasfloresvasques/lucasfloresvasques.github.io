The Social Life of Flowers: The Classification of Things and the Authority of Those Who Judge

PhD thesis in Social Sciences, UNESP, Araraquara, supervised by Maria Aparecida Chaves Jardim. Research stay at IDHE.S, ENS Paris-Saclay, with Frédéric Lebaron (FAPESP BEPE). Defence scheduled for March 2027.

Abstract

This thesis investigates how what counts in a plant is established, how people know and appreciate it, and in which relations their judgements guide decisions. In dialogue with Durkheim, Mauss and Bourdieu, it brings together historical documents, Brazilian and Dutch commercial standards, digital publications, a questionnaire with 72 participants, interviews and ethnographic records from Expoflora. Documentary and narrative analysis is combined with Geometric Data Analysis in the study of vocabularies and repertoires. In the standards, a commercial category allows differences among the plants in a lot. Distinguishing what is examined, what is measured and which limit is accepted clarifies this equivalence and the responsibilities for selection, reporting and correction. In historical documents, names and compositions participate in the allocation of credit and professional rights. On digital platforms, recommendations relate plant properties to uses and recipients; lexical analysis did not support the general oppositions expected between principles of judgement and professional positions. In the questionnaire, liking a group coexists with being unfamiliar with some of its examples. Declared familiarity contributes to differences among responses, whose interpretation changes with the list and the denominators. Comparison with films and music situates these repertoires among the same participants; the tests did not detect the family associations investigated. In accounts of cultivation, provenance and care provide reasons for keeping specimens similar to others. At the festival, the preparation of products, environments and fragments relates to different ways of participating and keeping mementos. We show how judgement relates to responsibilities for selecting, correcting and offering, and why knowing, appreciating and deciding to keep plants require distinct explanations. The sociology of classifications gains precision by examining objects and the scope of interventions.

Keywords: social classifications; ornamental plants; markets; taste; authority. Abstract as of October 2026.
